This letter serves as my formal appeal of BBB AUTO LINE’s ineligibility determination due to its reliance on the recent California Supreme Court decision in Rodriguez v. FCA US, LLC, S274625 (2024). While I respect the program’s adherence to the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act (the “Act”), I contend that the denial misapplies Rodriguez and overlooks the continuing obligations of Jaguar Land Rover North America, LLC (“JLRNA”) under federal and state warranty laws where active manufacturer warranties remain in effect. Furthermore, this appeal highlights compliance with the Arbitration Rules, particularly Rule 3 (Jurisdiction of BBB AUTO LINE), and demonstrates that the exclusions listed under “Claims That Will Not Be Arbitrated” do not apply to my case.

First, pursuant to Rule 3(a), my claim arises under Jaguar Land Rover North America’s (JLRNA) written warranty for my 2020 Jaguar F-Type, which was active at the time of my purchase on September 17, 2024, which JLRNA continued to honor through multiple repair attempts. JLRNA's acceptance of warranty obligations and performance of repairs under warranty terms establishes their ongoing duty to provide warranty coverage per 15 U.S.C. § 2310(d). JLRNA's warranty obligations continue regardless of subsequent ownership, as established in Duick v. Toyota Motor Sales, U.S.A., Inc., 198 Cal.App.4th 1316 (2011). This creates an independent basis for BBB Auto Line jurisdiction separate from Song-Beverly Act eligibility.

Second, Rule 3(b) grants jurisdiction to BBB AUTO LINE for disputes under the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 2301-2312). Per the Magnuson-Moss Act, manufacturers like JLRNA cannot disclaim their obligations to repair or replace defective warrantied goods when “substantial nonconformities” cannot be rectified after a reasonable number of repair attempts. My case squarely fits this criterion: the vehicle’s electrical and drivetrain issues were presented for repair multiple times within the warranty period (documentation included) without resolution, as required under 15 U.S.C. § 2304(a)(4).

In Rodriguez v. FCA US, LLC, the California Supreme Court clarified that a vehicle purchased with an unexpired manufacturer’s new car warranty does not qualify as being sold “with a manufacturer’s new car warranty” under Song-Beverly unless the new car warranty was issued contemporaneously with the

sale. However, the Court expressly limited its ruling to eligibility for Song-Beverly “new vehicle” remedies, such as buyback or replacement, and explicitly preserved other warranty rights where defects arise within an active manufacturer’s warranty period. Per Rodriguez, “[T]he decision does not eliminate a buyer’s ability to pursue other remedies when defects manifest under a manufacturer’s warranty,” including claims under the federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (15 U.S.C. §§ 2301– 2312) and related California Commercial Code provisions.

In addition to the federal warranty, California state law provides additional protections for defects arising in vehicles sold as “used” but still covered under active warranties. California Commercial Code § 2313 preserves my right to enforce express warranties made by the manufacturer, and Civil Code § 1795.5(c) extends implied warranty protections to subsequent buyers of vehicles covered by a manufacturer’s warranty.

Given the vehicle’s repair history under Jaguar’s transferable warranty and the continued presence of unresolved defects that impair its use, value, and safety, I respectfully request that the BBB Auto Line arbitrator reverse the eligibility determination and exercise jurisdiction over this matter under federal warranty law and JLRNA's express delegation of warranty disputes to this forum as the platform remains the most efficient and equitable venue to resolve this ongoing dispute.
