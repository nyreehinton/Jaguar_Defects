# Litigation History of Jag Dealers

Research indicates that both businesses have faced litigation, often related to consumer warranty issues under California's Song-Beverly Act (commonly known as lemon law cases), vehicle sales practices, and broader corporate settlements. Evidence suggests these are typical for auto dealerships, with no unusually high volume or patterns of severe misconduct noted across sources. However, some cases involve significant awards or settlements, highlighting potential risks in warranty fulfillment and title transfers. Outcomes vary, with several settlements or dismissals favoring defendants.

This location appears in multiple cases, frequently as a co-defendant with Jaguar Land Rover North America (JLRNA). Common themes include warranty breaches and negligent repairs, with one notable product liability case. The business was part of a statewide consumer protection settlement in 2025.

#### Overview of Jaguar Land Rover Woodland Hills (Terry York Motor Cars Ltd.)
Terry York Motor Cars Ltd., operating as Jaguar Land Rover Woodland Hills (and previously under DBAs like Land Rover Encino), has been involved in litigation dating back to the 1980s. As a subsidiary of AutoNation Inc., it has faced suits ranging from individual consumer claims to statewide enforcement actions. Many cases involve co-defendants like JLRNA, highlighting franchise relationships where dealers handle sales and repairs but manufacturers often share liability for defects.

Key historical context includes an early dispute with the Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) over retail practices, setting a precedent for regulatory scrutiny. More recent cases focus on lemon law violations, where plaintiffs allege unrepaired defects in Land Rover vehicles, leading to breaches of implied warranties. Product liability suits, though rarer, have resulted in substantial awards when defects are linked to injuries. In 2025, the business was implicated in a multi-district attorney settlement for systemic failures in vehicle registration processes, affecting consumer credit and tax obligations.
